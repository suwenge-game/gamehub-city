Streak strategy guide

Protect the streak by protecting the solve. Play the dated puzzle on consecutive calendar days, use early guesses for information, and become more conservative when only one or two rows remain.

How streak tracking works

The game records progress and statistics in localStorage in your browser. A daily win on consecutive calendar dates extends the current streak. A missed or lost daily breaks that sequence. Your best streak records the longest sequence reached on this browser even after the current count resets.

Local storage is private to the browser profile. Moving from a phone to a laptop, using a private window, clearing site data, or switching browsers can produce a separate record. No account synchronizes those histories. If continuity matters, play the daily puzzle in the same regular browser.

Conservative play versus a low guess count

A low-row solve is satisfying, but a streak rewards reliability rather than speed. On rows one through three, broad coverage may be appropriate. Once the board exposes a nearly complete pattern, list every plausible ending before choosing. If several answers differ by one letter, use a probe word only while enough rows remain to benefit from it.

For example, if the pattern is _ATCH and several initial letters remain possible, guessing one candidate at a time can consume the board. An information guess that tests several possible initials may be safer on row four. On row six, there is no future row to inform, so choose the best answer candidate instead.

Decisions on guesses five and six

Pause before row five. Recheck every gold tile’s forbidden position, every fixed green, and every gray letter. Account for duplicates: one gray copy beside one green copy may mean the answer contains exactly one instance, not zero. Then ask whether your proposed word could genuinely be the answer.

Row five can still be diagnostic when it separates two or more strong candidates and leaves a final solve. Row six cannot be diagnostic. It must obey all clues and should favor ordinary answer vocabulary over an obscure but merely accepted guess.

Manage the pressure

A long streak can make a routine board feel unusually important. Slow down instead of changing your method. Type a candidate, check it from left to right, and only then submit. Taking a short break is useful if you are repeatedly rearranging the same letters. The calendar puzzle waits; an impulsive Enter cannot be undone.

After a failure

A loss resets the current sequence, not your ability. The best-streak value remains a record of previous consistency. Review the board for one actionable error: a repeated gray, a misplaced gold, an overlooked double, or a risky candidate played too late. Then begin the next date at one. Treating the reset as feedback keeps one result from distorting future play.

Read the distribution

The guess distribution counts wins by the row on which the answer was found. A taller bar at four means many successful games ended on guess four; it is not a penalty. Played and win percentage describe completion and success, while current and best streak describe calendar consistency. These measures answer different questions, so do not sacrifice a likely six-row win merely to protect an attractive distribution.

Streak FAQ

Where is my streak stored?

It stays in localStorage for the current browser profile and does not automatically move between devices.

Do practice games extend it?

No. Dated daily wins extend the calendar streak.

What happens after a loss?

The current count resets, the best count remains, and the next dated puzzle can start a new streak.

Applying this page to a live solve

Every principle on this page compresses into a two-second board check. Take the page's core rule and translate it into a question you can ask before each swipe: for rules pages, "which constraint am I about to satisfy?"; for strategy pages, "which information does this move buy?"; for stats pages, "what does my number actually measure?" The translation habit matters more than the rule itself, because rules remembered as slogans get applied in the wrong phase of the game.

The archive pairs with this page. Open any recent day, play to the point where the page's principle fires, and compare your decision with the one the page recommends. Ten days of that pairing converts reading into reflex — which is the whole point of a strategy library rather than a strategy listicle.

A note on skill transfer: the principles here were measured on this site's answer bank, and the bank is what your daily puzzle draws from. Advice from generic word-game sites usually still points in the right direction, but its numbers drift — ours is regenerated whenever the bank changes, and each page shows its own recomputed figures. When this page and another site disagree, check which one is looking at the same word list you are actually playing.

Common misreadings of this material

First misreading: treating the page's strongest case as the only case. Strategy principles are priors, not laws — the board state overrides them whenever the feedback is unusual. The guides are calibrated for typical boards, and "typical" covers most but not all days.

Second misreading: skipping to the endgame material because it feels advanced. The endgame techniques assume the opening discipline is already automatic; applying narrow-board thinking to a wide-open board wastes the information advantage the early game just built. Sequence matters — the reading order in the strategy school is the dependency order.

Third misreading: optimizing for score at the expense of solve length. Long streaks come from consistent four-row solves, not occasional two-row wins followed by duds. If your average solve length is rising while your best solve length stays flat, the plan layer (opening pairs, anchor discipline) is where the leak is — not in the finishes.
