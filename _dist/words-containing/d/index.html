What this list is

Every entry below is a five-letter word that has appeared as a daily answer on this site. That makes the list materially different from a dictionary dump: it is the closed set your puzzle can actually pick from, filtered to words containing D. The percentage is exact — 120 of 599 answers (20%) — not a Scrabble-tile approximation.

Letter profile of D

Across the bank, D appears most often in slot 5 (9%) and slot 1 (4%). The average word on this list carries 1.91 vowel sounds, and 3 of the 120 entries contain D twice — a detail that matters because two-yellows boards are the classic misread (see the repeated-letters deep dive). 8 of these words also contain a Y, worth remembering when your candidate pool narrows.

Using this list at the board

When your feedback proves D is present, this table becomes your candidate pool. The second column (vowel count) is a fast tiebreaker — vowel-rich candidates split the pool more evenly when the remaining slots are untested.

If D appeared as a yellow rather than a green, strike the tested position from consideration and prefer entries where the remaining placements of D differ — each distinct placement is a separate hypothesis worth one probe, not one guess.

Feedback playbook for this pattern

Green-lock reading. Once this pattern is confirmed, treat the table above as the entire game: 120 candidates, D/B/A-leading in most cases, closing on D/S. The vowel distribution is 2 vowels (80 words), 1 vowels (26 words), which decides your probe style — vowel-sparse patterns want letter probes, vowel-rich patterns want position probes.

Yellow handling inside the pattern. A yellow on a letter that the pattern itself contains (like D appearing twice) means a double, not a miss — re-read the two-yellows rule before discarding candidates. 8 of these words carry a Y and 9 carry a W, the two letters solvers forget to test inside locked families.

Commit logic. When the remaining candidates fit on one hand, the two-away commit rule applies: prefer your leading word over a disambiguating probe unless the probe tests untried letters. The archive lets you rehearse exactly this decision on past boards that ended in this pattern.
