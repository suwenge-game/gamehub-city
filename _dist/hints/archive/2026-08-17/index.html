Making this material stick

Strategy reading decays fast without a retrieval habit, so pair this page with a concrete retrieval loop: after your next solve, close the page and write the one rule from here that applied to your board. If nothing applied, that is a data point too — it means the page's scope did not match your board's phase, and scope-matching is itself a skill this site teaches.

The second habit is a weekly audit. Pick your worst solve of the week — not your best — and locate the row where the plan diverged from the board. The divergence row almost always matches one of three failure classes: an unmapped yellow, an unnamed direction press, or a premature commit. Each class has a page here; the audit's job is routing you to it.

Third: share your reasoning, not your results. The score grid is a weak signal and a strong temptation; the reasoning chain is the actual skill artifact. Players who can explain a four-row solve in four sentences are the ones who keep improving — explanation forces the model of the board to stay explicit, and explicit models are correctable models.

Monday method: opener grading

Mondays are for reopening discipline. Before your first guess, grade your planned opener against three questions: does it hold two letters from the measured top eight, does it avoid reusing last week's opener, and does it place those letters in their statistically strongest slots? Grade honestly — most openers score two of three. The one you fail most often is the habit your Monday games should work on; the rest of the week can leave it alone.

The grading habit works because it converts opener choice from ritual into analysis. Ritual openers drift — small keyboard or gesture habits creep in over months until the word you type is not the word you meant to study. A Monday grade sheet (three columns, one line) catches drift the week it starts.

Monday's companion drill is the opener grade sheet from the strategy lab — three columns, ten games, and your opener drift is measured. Pair today's hints with that sheet: the difficulty profile above tells you whether the week's first board rewards coverage or elimination, and the grade sheet records which style your opener actually delivered.
