What this list is

Every entry below is a five-letter word that has appeared as a daily answer on this site. That makes the list materially different from a dictionary dump: it is the closed set your puzzle can actually pick from, filtered to words containing N. The percentage is exact — 146 of 599 answers (24%) — not a Scrabble-tile approximation.

Across the bank, N appears most often in slot 4 (9%) and slot 3 (6%). Using this list at the board

When your feedback proves N is present, this table becomes your candidate pool. Rank the survivors by what each would teach: words whose other letters sit in their most common slots buy more positional information per guess. The second column (vowel count) is a fast tiebreaker — vowel-rich candidates split the pool more evenly when the remaining slots are untested.

If N appeared as a yellow rather than a green, strike the tested position from consideration and prefer entries where the remaining placements of N differ — each distinct placement is a separate hypothesis worth one probe, not one guess.

Feedback playbook for this pattern

Green-lock reading. Once this pattern is confirmed, treat the table above as the entire game: 146 candidates, S/B/N-leading in most cases, closing on N/S. The vowel distribution is 2 vowels (89 words), 1 vowels (45 words), which decides your probe style — vowel-sparse patterns want letter probes, vowel-rich patterns want position probes.

Yellow handling inside the pattern. A yellow on a letter that the pattern itself contains (like N appearing twice) means a double, not a miss — re-read the two-yellows rule before discarding candidates. 10 of these words carry a Y and 15 carry a W, the two letters solvers forget to test inside locked families.

Commit logic. When the remaining candidates fit on one hand, the two-away commit rule applies: prefer your leading word over a disambiguating probe unless the probe tests untried letters. The archive lets you rehearse exactly this decision on past boards that ended in this pattern.
